# 0. Before you start

Two things decide whether your video can stay online. Sort them out before you film.

## 1. Which book

A read-aloud shows the whole book: every picture and every word. For a book someone else published, that is copying
their work, and the publisher can have the video removed or your channel penalised. "Giving credit" does not change
that, and neither does a small audience.

Use this kit with:

- **A book you wrote and illustrated yourself,** or your family made.
- **A public-domain book.** Check the rules for your country; the pictures must be public domain too, not only the words.
- **A book you have written permission for.** Many publishers have a page about read-aloud permissions, often with
  conditions (a time limit, a credit line, no monetisation). Keep the reply.

If you are unsure, ask the publisher before you film. This kit does not include any book, and nothing in it gives you
rights to one.

## 2. If the reader is a child

- A parent or guardian decides, films, uploads and runs the channel. The account belongs to an adult.
- Use a first name only. Keep school names, your street, uniforms and anything showing where you live out of the frame.
- Stop filming when the child stops enjoying it.
- On YouTube, videos made for children must be marked **"Yes, it's made for kids"**. That turns comments and
  notifications off; that is normal, leave it.
- Read every comment setting and privacy setting on each platform yourself.

## What you need

- A phone and a plain backdrop (a sheet pulled flat), with even light
- An Apple Silicon Mac (the speech step was tested there), `ffmpeg`, Python 3.11 or newer
- Photos or scans of each page of the book

It costs nothing to run; everything happens on your computer. A 9-minute read-aloud takes roughly an hour of
computer time, most of it the cutout step.
